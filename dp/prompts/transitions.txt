Show one row of letter boxes for the string "aabaa" (5 boxes).
Two pointers on this SAME row:
  "left" arrow pointing DOWN at a letter from ABOVE the row (pointing at a left-side letter).
  "right" arrow pointing DOWN at a letter from ABOVE the row (pointing at a right-side letter).
Both pointers are on the SAME string. This is NOT two separate strings.

SECTION 1: "MATCH (ends are equal)" (GREEN card, about 50%):

Show the string "aabaa" with:
  left pointing at index 0 ('a') on the LEFT side
  right pointing at index 4 ('a') on the RIGHT side
  'a' == 'a', they match!

  Action: "Ends match. Peel off both. Check inner."
  Cost: None. Match just means this layer is valid.
  left: left -> left+1 "Confirmed left end. Move inward."
  right: right -> right-1 "Confirmed right end. Move inward."
  Call: is_palindrome(left+1, right-1)

  Show curved GREEN arrows: left arrow moves RIGHT (inward), right arrow moves LEFT (inward).
  Label: "Both pointers move INWARD. Palindrome shrinks from both sides."

  "Why both change? Palindrome is symmetric. We check both ends at once."

SECTION 2: "NO MATCH (ends differ)" (RED card, about 30%):

Show a different string example like "abc" with:
  left pointing at 'a' (index 0)
  right pointing at 'c' (index 2)
  'a' != 'c'

  Action: "Ends differ. NOT a palindrome."
  Result: Return False. No recursion.
  No arrows. Just a big X or stop sign.
  "If outer chars differ, no inner arrangement can fix it."

Kit at bottom (about 20%): "left moves right, right moves left. They squeeze inward until they meet or cross."

Gold box: "Base case: left >= right. Single char or empty = always a palindrome."

Create a tall portrait infographic (1024x1792). Three sections.

Big header at top: "WHY DOES EACH VARIABLE CHANGE?"

CRITICAL LAYOUT: In EVERY section, show TWO SEPARATE ROWS of letter boxes:
  TOP ROW labeled "text1:" showing letters a  b  c  d  e  (5 boxes).
  BOTTOM ROW labeled "text2:" showing letters a  c  e  (3 boxes).
  Pointer "i" is an ARROW pointing DOWN at a letter in the TOP ROW (text1).
  Pointer "j" is an ARROW pointing DOWN at a letter in the BOTTOM ROW (text2).
  "i" NEVER appears on the bottom row. "j" NEVER appears on the top row.

SECTION 1: "MATCH" (GREEN card, about 35%):

Show text1 with i pointing at 'a' (index 0) on the TOP ROW.
Show text2 with j pointing at 'a' (index 0) on the BOTTOM ROW.
'a' == 'a', they match!

  Action: "Characters match. Take it. Part of LCS."
  Reward: +1. Found one common character.
  i on text1: i -> i+1 "text1[i] consumed (it matched)"
  j on text2: j -> j+1 "text2[j] consumed (same character)"
  Call: 1 + lcs(i+1, j+1)
  "Both advance: match consumes one from each string."

SECTION 2: "SKIP text1[i]" (AMBER card, about 30%):

Show text1 with i pointing at 'b' (index 1) on the TOP ROW.
Show text2 with j pointing at 'c' (index 1) on the BOTTOM ROW.
'b' != 'c'. Skip text1[i].

  Action: "Skip text1[i]. Maybe later text1 char matches."
  Reward: +0. Skipping adds nothing.
  i on text1: i -> i+1 "text1[i] skipped. Move past."
  j on text2: j STAYS "text2[j] still waiting to be matched."
  Call: lcs(i+1, j)
  "Only i moves. Skipping text1 doesn't help text2."

SECTION 3: "SKIP text2[j]" (AMBER card, about 25%):

Show text1 with i pointing at 'b' (index 1) on the TOP ROW.
Show text2 with j pointing at 'c' (index 1) on the BOTTOM ROW.
'b' != 'c'. Skip text2[j].

  Action: "Skip text2[j]. Maybe text1[i] matches later."
  Reward: +0.
  i on text1: i STAYS "text1[i] still available."
  j on text2: j -> j+1 "text2[j] skipped. Move past."
  Call: lcs(i, j+1)
  "Only j moves. Skipping text2 doesn't help text1."

Kit at bottom: "Match = both advance. Skip = only the skipped string's pointer moves.
Decision: max(skip_first, skip_second). Want the LONGEST."

  c: c -> c+1 "Moved right one column."
  Call: count_paths(r, c+1)
  "Only c changes. Right = horizontal = column changes."

Kit at bottom (about 20%): "down + right = total paths. We ADD because we COUNT all paths, not pick the best."

Gold box: "Base case: reached bottom-right = 1 path. Out of bounds = 0 paths."

  j on s2: j -> j+1 "s2[j] consumed. It produced the needed s3 char."

  "Only j moves. We took from s2, not s1."

Create a tall portrait infographic (1024x1792). Three sections.

Big header at top: "WHY DOES EACH VARIABLE CHANGE?"

CRITICAL LAYOUT: In EVERY section, show TWO SEPARATE ROWS of letter boxes:
  TOP ROW labeled "s:" showing letters r  a  b  b  b  i  t  (7 boxes).
  BOTTOM ROW labeled "t:" showing letters r  a  b  i  t  (5 boxes).
  Pointer "i" is an ARROW pointing DOWN at a letter in the TOP ROW (s).
  Pointer "j" is an ARROW pointing DOWN at a letter in the BOTTOM ROW (t).
  "i" NEVER appears on the bottom row. "j" NEVER appears on the top row.

SECTION 1: "MATCH + USE" (GREEN card, about 35%):

Show s with i pointing at 'r' (index 0) on the TOP ROW.
Show t with j pointing at 'r' (index 0) on the BOTTOM ROW.
's' == 'r', they match! Choose to USE it.

  Action: "Use s[i] to match t[j]."
  i on s: i -> i+1 "s[i] consumed (used for the match)."
  j on t: j -> j+1 "t[j] matched (covered by s[i])."
  Call: count_subsequences(i+1, j+1)
  "Both advance: using s[i] to match t[j] consumes one from each."

SECTION 2: "MATCH + SKIP" (AMBER card, about 30%):

Show s with i pointing at 'b' (index 2) on the TOP ROW.
Show t with j pointing at 'b' (index 2) on the BOTTOM ROW.
They match! But choose to SKIP s[i].

  Action: "Skip s[i] even though it matches. Maybe a later copy gives more paths."
  i on s: i -> i+1 "s[i] skipped. Move past it."
  j on t: j STAYS "t[j] not matched yet. Still waiting."
  Call: count_subsequences(i+1, j)
  "Only i moves. We chose not to use this match."

  "Why skip a match? s has 'bbb' but t only needs one 'b'. Each copy is a different subsequence."

SECTION 3: "NO MATCH" (RED card, about 25%):

Show s with i pointing at 'b' (index 2) on the TOP ROW.
Show t with j pointing at 'i' (index 3) on the BOTTOM ROW.
'b' != 'i'. No match.

  Action: "s[i] can't match t[j]. Skip it."
  i on s: i -> i+1 "s[i] skipped (useless for current target)."
  j on t: j STAYS "t[j] still waiting to be matched."
  Call: count_subsequences(i+1, j)

Kit at bottom: "Match: use + skip (ADD both counts). No match: skip only.
This is knapsack's 'grab + skip' on strings."

SECTION 1: "FROM REST" (BLUE card, about 35%):

Arrow 1 (to HOLD, red arrow):
  BUY: "Buy stock at today's price."
  Cost: -prices[day] "Money spent."
  day: day -> day+1 "Move to next day."
  state: rest -> hold "Now own a stock."
  Call: -prices[day] + max_profit(day+1, "hold")

Arrow 2 (loops back to REST, blue arrow):
  WAIT: "Do nothing."
  Cost: +0
  day: day -> day+1
  state: stays rest "Still free."
  Call: max_profit(day+1, "rest")

Decision: max(buy, wait)

SECTION 2: "FROM HOLD" (RED card, about 35%):

Show the HOLD circle with two outgoing arrows:

Arrow 1 (to SOLD, green arrow):
  SELL: "Sell stock at today's price."
  Reward: +prices[day] "Money received."
  day: day -> day+1
  state: hold -> sold "Must cooldown next."
  Call: prices[day] + max_profit(day+1, "sold")
  "Why sold, not rest? Cooldown rule: must wait one day after selling."

Arrow 2 (loops back to HOLD, red arrow):
  KEEP: "Hold the stock."
  Cost: +0
  day: day -> day+1
  state: stays hold
  Call: max_profit(day+1, "hold")

Decision: max(sell, keep)

SECTION 3: "FROM SOLD" (GREEN card, about 20%):

Show the SOLD circle with one outgoing arrow:

Arrow (to REST, blue arrow):
  COOLDOWN: "Mandatory rest day."
  Cost: +0
  day: day -> day+1
  state: sold -> rest "Cooldown complete."
  Call: max_profit(day+1, "rest")
  "No choice. The 'sold' state exists to enforce the one-day gap."

Kit at bottom: "The constraint creates the states. Each state tells you what you're allowed to do next."

Create a tall portrait infographic (1024x1792). Two sections.

Big header at top: "HOW DOES THE CHAIN EXTEND?"

Show ONE row of number boxes:
  nums: 10  9  2  5  3  7  101  18
  Show index labels below each: 0  1  2  3  4  5  6  7

SECTION 1: "EXTEND (nums[prev] < nums[index])" (GREEN card, about 55%):

Highlight index = 5 (value 7) with a GOLD border. This is "us".
Show arrows from ALL valid prev values:
  prev=2 (value 2): 2 < 7, GREEN arrow from 2 to 7. lis[2]=1, so 1+1=2.
  prev=3 (value 5): 5 < 7, GREEN arrow from 5 to 7. lis[3]=2, so 2+1=3.
  prev=4 (value 3): 3 < 7, GREEN arrow from 3 to 7. lis[4]=2, so 2+1=3.

Skip invalid prev values:
  prev=0 (value 10): 10 > 7, RED X. Can't extend. NOT increasing.
  prev=1 (value 9): 9 > 7, RED X.

  Action: "Extend the best chain ending at prev."
  Reward: +1. Adding nums[index] to the chain.
  Why extend? nums[prev] < nums[index]. Sequence stays increasing.
  Call: 1 + lis_ending_at(prev)

Best: max(2, 3, 3) = 3. lis[5] = 3. Chain: [2, 5, 7] or [2, 3, 7].

SECTION 2: "STANDALONE" (AMBER card, about 25%):

Show a single element standing alone with lis = 1.
  "No valid prev? Start a new chain. Length = 1."
  "Every element is an increasing subsequence of length 1 by itself."

Kit at bottom (about 20%): "Check ALL previous elements. Keep the longest valid extension.
Decision: best = max(1, max(1 + lis[prev] for all valid prev))."

PROMPT 8: Burst Balloons Transition Justification -> burst_balloons_transitions.png
================================================================================

---BEGIN STYLE BIBLE---
[Paste the full style bible from above here]
---END STYLE BIBLE---

Create a tall portrait infographic (1024x1792). Two sections.

Big header at top: "WHY BURST LAST, NOT FIRST?"

Show a row of balloon circles with values: [1]  3  1  5  8  [1]
The [1]s on the ends are boundary padding (shown with dashed outlines).
Label them: "boundary" on both sides.

SECTION 1: "THE KEY INSIGHT" (GOLD card, about 45%):

Show two scenarios side by side:

LEFT SIDE: "BURST FIRST (BAD)" (RED border)
  If we burst balloon 1 (value 3) FIRST:
  Remaining: [1]  1  5  8  [1]
  Now balloon 1 (value 1) has NEW neighbors. The subproblems are tangled.
  Label: "Neighbors shift! Subproblems depend on each other."

RIGHT SIDE: "BURST LAST (GOOD)" (GREEN border)
  If balloon k is burst LAST in [left, right]:
  Everything else is already gone.
  k's neighbors are nums[left-1] and nums[right+1] (boundaries).
  Label: "Boundaries are fixed! Subproblems are independent."

SECTION 2: "THE TRANSITION" (GREEN card, about 35%):

Show range [left=1, right=4] with balloons: 3  1  5  8
Choose k=3 (value 5) as LAST to burst.

  Reward: nums[left-1] * nums[k] * nums[right+1] = 1 * 5 * 1 = 5
  "When k is last, everything else inside is already gone."

  Left subproblem: [1, 2] = balloons 3, 1. Burst these before k.
  Right subproblem: [4, 4] = balloon 8. Burst this before k.
  "k is still standing as a boundary for both subproblems!"

  Call: 5 + max_coins(1, 2) + max_coins(4, 4)

  "Subproblems are independent: left side doesn't affect right side."

Kit at bottom (about 20%): "Try every k as last. Take the max.
The 'burst last' reversal is what makes the subproblems clean."
